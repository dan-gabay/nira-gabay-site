## Audience

- **Primary**: Hebrew-speaking parents of children and teenagers in Israel, reading
  at the moment a specific difficulty has become hard to ignore - the morning
  goodbye, the nightly screen argument, a report card, a child with no friends.
- **Secondary**: adults and couples looking for therapy for themselves. Reached
  mostly through the service pages rather than the blog.
- **Expertise**: beginner. No clinical vocabulary is assumed. Terms like
  ספרציה, CBT or אפר"ת are always unpacked on first use.
- **Arrival**: ~70% from Google organic. Facebook sends a small share and has
  never produced an enquiry. Assume a cold reader mid-search, not a follower.

### Active problems

- A child who cannot separate, cannot sleep, cannot be left.
- Sibling rivalry the parent reads as their own failure at fairness.
- A teenager who has gone quiet, stopped eating with the family, or lost friends.
- The screen argument that repeats every evening.
- Anxiety with a specific address: exams, social situations, uncertainty.
- Depression, OCD or addiction in the house, and not knowing when it stops being a phase.
- Co-parenting after divorce; a partner returning from reserve duty.
- "Am I the problem?" - which sits under almost all of the above.

### Common misconceptions to correct

These are the reframes the existing articles already make. They are the
information-gain spine of this blog.

- Sibling rivalry is a parenting failure. **It is not** - and equal division is
  not the fix; certainty of place is.
- Separation anxiety means something went wrong. **It means attachment formed.**
- "You're not fat" is a reassuring answer. **It ends the conversation.**
- The screen problem is the number of minutes. **It is the unclear boundary.**
- Depression is a character weakness or a passing mood. **It is a disorder.**
- A child who erupts is badly raised. **They were cut off mid-experience.**

**Distinctive POV - "ההתנהגות סבירה"**

The child's behaviour is a reasonable response to a real difficulty, not a
defect to be corrected. The symptom is the most logical solution the child has
found so far. `ילד שמתפרץ ברגע שכיביתם את הטלוויזיה אינו ילד לא מחונך, אלא ילד

## Editorial Rules

### Always do

- Open on something a person actually said, or a scene they recognise.
- Relieve the guilt within the first two paragraphs.
- Explain the mechanism before offering the action.
- Pair every "what helps" list with a "what does not help" list.
- Give the parent the exact sentence to say, in quotation marks.
- Close by normalising, then invite once, quietly.
- Mark any case study as `(שם בדוי)` and change identifying detail.

### Never do

- Never invent a statistic, a percentage, a study or an expert. The corpus
  contains **zero digits**; that is a standard, not an accident.
- Never promise an outcome or a timeframe for therapy.
- Never tell the reader they did it wrong.
- Never write a hard CTA. One low-commitment sentence at the end.
- Never claim a diagnosis, and never suggest or discuss specific medication.
- Never write outside the treatment scope below in the clinical first person.
- Never use an em dash or en dash; regular hyphen only.
- Never use an exclamation mark.

### Taboo phrases

- `חמישה טיפים` / `X דרכים` and any listicle framing
- `הדרך הנכונה` / `השיטה שלי` / `הסוד ל`
- `מובטח` / `תוך X מפגשים` / any outcome promise
- `הורים מושלמים` as an aspiration rather than something to release
- `אנרגיות`, `ריפוי`, `יקום` and the surrounding register

### Required disclosures

**High-risk articles only** - depression, addiction, anxiety disorders, OCD,
eating disorders, self-harm, and anything touching suicidal ideation. On those,
close with a plain line that the article is not a substitute for diagnosis or
treatment, plus an emergency contact (ער"ן 1201).

Everyday parenting articles - siblings, screens, routines, report cards - carry
no disclosure. Adding one everywhere would flatten the warmth for no gain.

**Current state: no article carries a disclosure today.** This is the one
open E-E-A-T and safety gap in an otherwise strong YMYL setup.

### In scope - first-person clinical voice allowed

- **הפרעות אכילה** · **טראומה ופוסט-טראומה** · **התמכרויות**

`כשילד מגיע אליי...` and `בטיפול אנחנו עובדים על...` are available here, and
nowhere else.

### Partial scope - explain and refer, no clinical first person

Adjacent difficulties she does not treat directly. Cover the mechanism and what
a parent can do, then point onward. Never imply she takes these cases.

### Out of scope

- Diagnosis of any kind, and anything requiring psychiatric authority.
- Medication: naming, dosing, comparing, or advising on starting or stopping.
- Acute crisis management as instructions. Direct to emergency services instead.
